# Difficult customer conversations

Anger in support is usually about the impact of the problem rather than
about the agent, but it arrives pointed at the agent. Handling it means
addressing the impact directly while staying honest about what can and
cannot be done.

## Method

1. **Acknowledge the impact before the detail.** Naming what it cost
   them, specifically, defuses more than any apology formula, because it
   proves you understood.
2. **Do not mirror the escalation.** Matching intensity guarantees
   escalation; a steady, specific tone gives the conversation somewhere
   to go (see difficult-emails).
3. **Be direct about what you cannot do.** Softening a refusal into
   ambiguity prolongs the conflict and reads as evasion once understood.
4. **Offer the real options, including the unwelcome ones.** A refund, a
   workaround, or an escalation with a date beats an open-ended we will
   look into it.
5. **Separate the person from the behaviour.** Abuse is not something an
   agent must absorb, and a clear policy for ending abusive contact
   protects staff.
6. **Move channel when it will help.** A call resolves in minutes what
   an email thread inflames over days, particularly where tone is being
   misread.
7. **Document factually.** Notes that record what happened rather than
   characterising the customer stay useful and defensible.

## Boundaries

De-escalation techniques do not make an unreasonable demand reasonable,
and some conversations end without agreement. Agents need authority to
resolve or the conversation stalls regardless of skill. Repeated
distress is a wellbeing matter for the team, not a training gap.
