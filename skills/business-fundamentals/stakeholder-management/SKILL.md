# Stakeholder management

Decisions get reopened because someone with influence was not consulted
and finds out late. Stakeholder management is unglamorous and prevents
most of the rework that plagues cross-functional projects.

## Method

1. **Map who is affected and who can block.** Influence and interest are
   different axes, and the person who can stop you is not always the one
   most affected.
2. **Understand what each actually needs.** Not their stated position
   but the outcome they are accountable for, which is usually
   satisfiable without giving them what they asked for (see
   negotiation-fundamentals).
3. **Consult before deciding, inform after.** Consultation after a
   decision is theatre and is recognised as such, which costs more trust
   than not consulting.
4. **Match communication to stake.** Deeply affected stakeholders need
   conversation; peripheral ones need a summary. Treating everyone alike
   overwhelms some and neglects others (see status-updates).
5. **Surface disagreement early and explicitly.** Unresolved objections
   resurface at the worst moment, and naming them lets them be addressed
   while it is cheap.
6. **Record decisions and who was consulted.** It ends the relitigation
   that consumes cross-functional projects (see agent-decision-log).
7. **Keep them updated through delivery.** Alignment decays, and silence
   is interpreted as either failure or exclusion.

## Boundaries

Management of stakeholders is not manipulation; the goal is genuine
alignment, and consultation that never changes anything is quickly seen
through. Some conflicts are real and must be escalated rather than
managed. Too many stakeholders is itself a structural problem (see
organizational-design).
