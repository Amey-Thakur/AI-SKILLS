# Positioning and messaging

Positioning is a decision about the market you compete in; messaging is
how you say it. Products described as everything to everyone are chosen
by nobody, because the buyer cannot tell whether it is for them.

## Method

1. **Name the alternative you are replacing.** Positioning is relative,
   and the honest alternative is often a spreadsheet or doing nothing
   rather than a competitor (see competitive-strategy).
2. **Pick one primary audience.** The message that resonates with a
   specific buyer beats the one that mildly interests everyone.
3. **Lead with the outcome, not the mechanism.** Buyers care what
   changes for them; the technology matters only as evidence it works.
4. **Use the customer's words.** Language taken from interviews and
   support tickets converts better than internal vocabulary, which
   describes the product rather than the problem (see
   customer-interviews).
5. **State one differentiator you can defend.** A claim any competitor
   could make is not positioning, and three differentiators are none.
6. **Write the one-sentence version and use it everywhere.** Site,
   deck, and description agreeing is what makes a product feel
   coherent (see agent-brand-guardian).
7. **Test it on someone unfamiliar.** If they cannot repeat back who it
   is for and why it is better, the messaging is not working yet.

## Boundaries

Positioning shapes perception; it cannot make a product fit a market it
does not serve. It requires saying no to segments, which is
uncomfortable and is the entire point. Repositioning an established
product is expensive and confuses existing customers.
