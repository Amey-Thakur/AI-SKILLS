# Agent accountability loop

Most work does not fail; it fades. Nobody refuses, the date passes,
attention moves, and three weeks later somebody asks what happened to
that. An accountability loop is the mechanical answer: every commitment
tracked to an outcome, with chasing that does not depend on anyone
remembering.

## Method

1. **Record commitments where they are made.** Every meeting, briefing,
   and assignment produces items with an owner and a date, captured at
   the moment rather than reconstructed later (see
   agent-meeting-runner).
2. **Chase before the deadline, not after.** A check shortly before the
   date catches the slip while it is still recoverable, which is the
   whole value of the loop.
3. **Ask for the blocker, not the status.** Status invites a
   reassurance; asking what is in the way produces something actionable.
4. **Escalate on a ladder, not on frustration.** A missed date is a
   reminder, a second is a flag to the chief of staff, a third is a
   decision about whether the work should exist (see
   agent-escalation-ladder).
5. **Allow renegotiation, once, explicitly.** Dates change for real
   reasons, and a renegotiated commitment with a new date is healthy
   where silent slippage is not.
6. **Close every item with an outcome.** Done, dropped, or superseded,
   recorded. Items that simply vanish teach everyone the loop is
   theatre.
7. **Report the pattern, not just the items.** Which kinds of work
   consistently slip is the signal worth acting on.

## Boundaries

The loop tracks and chases; it does not do the work or judge the worker.
Chasing people needs a human's tone and context, and an agent pinging a
person repeatedly is corrosive. Where commitments are consistently
missed the cause is usually capacity or clarity rather than will (see
agent-capacity-planning).
